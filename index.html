About this model

Terrain is real elevation data (USGS 3DEP / SRTM via AWS Terrain Tiles), shown with 1.5× vertical exaggeration. Rivers and creeks are traced from that terrain, and flooding uses the Height Above Nearest Drainage method that hydrologists use for rapid flood mapping. Temperatures cool about 3.5 °F per 1,000 ft of elevation, so ridges can get snow while the valley gets rain. Ridges also get extra rain or snow as air is forced up over them, and calm, clear nights trap cold air in the valleys. Rivers are modeled as linear reservoirs: creeks respond in minutes, the French Broad over a day.

Scenarios are reconstructions for teaching, not official records. The campus buildings are schematic unless OpenStreetMap data loads. Road data © OpenStreetMap contributors.
